# Visual Style Guide

## Design Direction

**Warm Editorial / Architectural Minimalism**

The website should feel like a thoughtfully designed independent publication or architect's portfolio: professional, clean, restrained and modern, while retaining warmth and personality.

The visual language combines:
- Minimal, editorial web design.
- Warm natural materials and muted earth tones.
- Fine architectural illustration and hand-drawn details.
- Clear typography, generous whitespace and understated interactions.

Avoid generic corporate portfolio aesthetics, bright tech colours, excessive gradients, rounded cards, heavy shadows and unnecessary decoration.

## Colour Palette

Use a restrained palette built around warm paper, charcoal, stone, sage and timber.

| Colour | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Warm Paper | `#F3F0E8` | Primary page background |
| Soft Charcoal | `#292B28` | Primary text, outlines and illustration linework |
| Stone | `#D8D1C3` | Borders, secondary surfaces and backgrounds |
| Sage | `#A2A18B` | Accent colour, highlights and selected states |
| Muted Timber | `#98785F` | Illustration details and natural material accents |
| Olive Grey | `#77796F` | Secondary text, metadata and subtle details |

### Colour Distribution

- 70% warm neutrals and whitespace.
- 20% charcoal and stone.
- 10% accent colours (sage and timber).

Use accent colours sparingly. The interface should remain predominantly neutral, with more expressive colour reserved for the illustrated room and its objects.

Avoid pure white and pure black where possible.

## Typography

Use three complementary typefaces.

| Typeface | Role | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Cormorant Garamond** | Editorial serif | Main headings, section titles and occasional featured text |
| **DM Sans** | Primary sans-serif | Body text, navigation, buttons and interface elements |
| **IBM Plex Mono** | Monospaced | Metadata, dates, coordinates, technical labels and numbering |

All fonts are available through Google Fonts.

### Typography Scale

| Element | Typeface | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Main headings | Cormorant Garamond | 36–52px |
| Section headings | Cormorant Garamond | 26–34px |
| Body text | DM Sans | 14–16px |
| Navigation | DM Sans | 12–13px |
| Metadata / labels | IBM Plex Mono | 10–11px |

Use regular or medium weights predominantly. Avoid excessive bolding; establish hierarchy through typography, spacing and contrast instead.

## Interface Styling

- **Background:** Warm Paper (`#F3F0E8`).
- **Text:** Soft Charcoal (`#292B28`).
- **Borders:** Thin, subtle Stone (`#D8D1C3`) lines.
- **Corners:** Mostly square, with minimal rounding (2–4px).
- **Shadows:** Restrained; primarily reserved for the illustrated light-box and physical depth effects.
- **Buttons:** Understated text links or simple outlined buttons.
- **Navigation:** Small, clean typography with subtle active-state indicators.
- **Spacing:** Generous whitespace. Avoid cramped layouts.
- **Layout:** Clear alignment, consistent spacing and restrained visual hierarchy.

Avoid excessive cards, pill-shaped buttons, thick borders and decorative UI elements.

## Illustration Style

The central interactive room/light-box is the website's primary visual feature.

Its aesthetic should be:
- Hand-drawn architectural illustration.
- Fine charcoal or pencil-like outlines.
- Subtle shading, cross-hatching and natural shadows.
- Muted, natural colours and understated textures.
- Layered physical depth, resembling a miniature room or architectural shadow box.
- Slightly imperfect and human, rather than overly polished or CAD-like.

Use timber, aged paper, muted sage and warm neutral tones to give objects material character.

The illustration should feel tactile and personal, while remaining visually coherent with the clean interface.

## Interaction Styling

Interactions should be subtle, purposeful and consistent.

- Hover states may use a slight colour shift, outline, opacity change or small movement.
- Interactive objects should have clear visual feedback.
- Selected objects should remain distinguishable from hover-only states.
- Transitions should be smooth and restrained.
- Avoid excessive animation, bouncing, scaling or distracting effects.
- Provide equivalent tap interactions for mobile devices.
- Maintain visible keyboard focus states and support reduced-motion preferences.

## Overall Principles

1. **Restraint:** Every visual element should have a purpose.
2. **Warmth:** Prefer natural, muted colours over cold greys and bright accents.
3. **Clarity:** Navigation and content must remain immediately understandable.
4. **Consistency:** Typography, spacing, colours and interaction patterns should be reused throughout.
5. **Character:** Allow the illustration and personal objects to provide individuality without compromising usability.
6. **Accessibility:** Maintain readable contrast, responsive layouts, keyboard accessibility and reduced-motion support.

The finished website should feel like a modern, carefully curated personal archive: part professional portfolio, part creative workspace, and part architectural illustration.
